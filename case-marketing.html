Designing the Spacebring website after its rebrand

Role
UI/UX designer
Scope
Website, component library
Year
2024 – 2026
Live website
Visit the site

Overview

Spacebring is a coworking management platform that helps operators run bookings, billing, access control and community from one place. Its website is where operators first meet the product, compare it with other tools and book a demo.

As a UI/UX designer, I designed the website after Spacebring’s rebrand: more than 20 pages, the images on them, a component library to build them from, and layouts for tablet and phone. I worked closely with developers and the marketing team, and after launch I kept extending the site and the library as the product grew.

Challenge

The rebrand gave Spacebring a new look, but only one page was designed in it: the home page. A home page answers few questions. It doesn’t show how a pricing table, a long feature page, a form or a blog should look. The rest of the site needed all of those answers quickly, and they had to hold up for every page that came after.

The blog was the hardest part. The marketing team had a clear idea of how it should work and which sections it needed. My job was to give them that while making it feel like part of the same website, not a separate one.

Approach

I designed sections, not pages. Each part of the home page became a component, and every new section I needed was added to the same library. That way more than 20 pages could be put together from one set of parts instead of being drawn one by one.

For the blog, I started from what the marketing team needed, then built those sections from the library’s type, spacing and components. They got the structure they asked for, and the blog stayed consistent with the rest of the site.

Images followed one style with four kinds, each picked for what a section had to explain: product screenshots to show the real interface, cropped close-ups to point at one feature, diagrams to explain how parts of the product connect, collages to set the mood of a page.

Outcome

The website launched with more than 20 pages, all built from one library and all in the new brand.

The library is still in use today. New pages are put together from existing sections, and the site has stayed consistent while it kept growing.
